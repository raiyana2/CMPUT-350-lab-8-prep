Apply the scale first and the translation second:
p' = p * scale + translation. This way, the translation itself is not scaled.

The rectangle's corners are (200,150), (600,150), (200,450) and (600,450).

The two counterclockwise triangles are:
(200,150), (200,450), (600,150)  and (600,150), (200,450), (600,450).

Store the four corners once and use indices 0,2,1,2,3,1 (in
top-left, top-right, bottom-left, bottom-right order). For this quad, that
uses 48 bytes for unindexed float positions, or 56 bytes for indexed
positions and indices. With double positions, the totals are 96 and 88 bytes.

In general, indexing is worthwhile when the space saved by reusing vertices
is greater than the space used by the indices. For N triangles and U unique
positions, that is 2S(3N-U) > 12N, where S is 4 bytes for floats or 8 bytes
for doubles.
